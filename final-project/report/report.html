Loan Offer Compass: An Auditable Comparison of Repayment Plans

Finance with AI Agents - Final Project Topic: Loans and financing (Appendix A) Kaiming Tian - HKUST IEDA - AI Agents and Financial Optimization 10 November 2026

Scope sentence. For a borrower comparing instalment-loan quotations, this project takes a CSV of offers plus a borrower affordability config, produces exact amortisation schedules, an IRR-based effective APR/EAR, an affordability verdict and a ranking, and is useful because headline nominal rates hide fees and term effects; I check it with a known-answer reference, an independent second implementation, cash-flow identities and changed-input cases.

HKUST IEDA · Finance with AI Agents · Final Project · 10 November 2026

1. The finance problem

A borrower shopping for a personal instalment loan receives quotations that are not directly comparable. One lender advertises a low nominal rate but charges an arrangement fee; another quotes a higher rate with no fees; a third offers a shorter term that lowers total interest but raises the monthly payment; a fourth uses an equal-principal plan whose payments decline over time. The borrower also has a hard monthly ceiling: an offer that is cheapest in total can be impossible to service.

User and task. An individual borrower (HKD 180,000, 36-month horizon in the primary case) who must choose among offers subject to a HKD 6,000 monthly budget cap and a 35% payment-to-income ceiling. The specific decision is: which offer has the lowest all-in cost among the ones the borrower can actually afford, and how much principal could they borrow before the ceiling binds?

Why this is useful. The usual approach - a hand-built spreadsheet or a "pick the lowest advertised rate" shortcut - is repetitive, error-prone and hard to verify. Common failure modes are: nominal rate confused with effective annual rate; fees omitted from the cost; a 24-month and a 36-month offer compared on total interest without regard to cash-flow timing; the first (largest) payment of an equal-principal plan used interchangeably with a level payment; and an offer ranked cheapest while being unaffordable. Each of these produces a number that looks plausible and is wrong.

Deliverable and smallest useful version. Inputs: one CSV of offers and one small JSON of borrower constraints. Outputs: a full schedule per offer, a comparison table, effective APR/EAR, an affordability verdict, a ranking and a machine-readable check record. The smallest useful version - three offers, one CSV in, one comparison table and one schedule out, with a known-answer check - was implemented first; the final version adds a second repayment structure, fees in two forms, a max-affordable-principal solver, charts, an independent checker and a reusable Skill package.

2.2 Financial definitions

Monthly periods; i = annual_nominal_rate / 12. Fees are assumed withheld from the disbursement at t = 0 (paying them separately at t = 0 gives the same net cash flow). From the borrower's point of view:

t = 0        + (principal - upfront_fee)
t = 1..n     - (interest_t + principal_t + monthly_fee)

interest_t   = opening_balance_t * i
closing_t    = opening_balance_t - principal_t
outflow_t    = interest_t + principal_t + monthly_fee
PMT          = principal * i / (1 - (1 + i)^-n)          [annuity, i > 0]
PMT          = principal / n                              [i = 0]

Two repayment structures are supported: annuity (level total payment) and equal_principal (constant principal, declining total payment). The final instalment clears the remaining balance exactly, so no residual dust is left.

The effective APR is the IRR of those cash flows, found by bisection: APR = 12 * i_IRR; EAR = (1 + i_IRR)^12 - 1. This is the only figure that puts rates, fees and terms on one footing. All-in cost is the nominal sum sum(outflows) - net_disbursement = total interest + upfront fee + n * monthly_fee; it is deliberately not discounted. Both are reported because they answer different questions and can rank offers differently.

Affordability uses the peak monthly outflow against the binding ceiling min(monthly_budget_cap, monthly_income * max_payment_to_income), plus the payment-to-income ratio. The peak (not the average) matters because an equal-principal plan is front-loaded. max_affordable_principal is found by bisection on the principal. The naive baseline - pick the lowest headline nominal rate - is recorded as the simple heuristic for comparison.

4.1 Primary case (Case A, HKD)

A1_LOW_RATE_FEE annuity 5.200% 36 5,410.94 4,500.00 14,793.82 19,293.82 6.900% 7.123% YES
A2_NO_FEE annuity 6.300% 36 5,500.45 0.00 18,016.18 18,016.18 6.300% 6.485% YES
A3_SHORT_TERM annuity 6.800% 24 8,042.75 0.00 13,026.09 13,026.09 6.800% 7.016% NO

All currency figures are HKD. The engine's seven internal identity checks pass 7/7 for every offer.

Finding 1 - the headline-rate trap. The naive baseline picks A1_LOW_RATE_FEE (5.200%, the lowest quoted rate). Its 2.5% arrangement fee (HKD 4,500 withheld from the disbursement) raises the effective APR to 6.900%, above the fee-free A2_NO_FEE at 6.300%. Ranking by effective APR gives A2, A4, A3, A1; the shortcut and the all-in ranking disagree. A borrower following the shortcut would pay HKD 1,277.64 more in all-in cost over three years (19,293.82 vs 18,016.18).

Finding 2 - cheapest ≠ affordable. A3_SHORT_TERM has the lowest all-in cost of all four offers (HKD 13,026.09) because 24 months accrue less interest, but its peak instalment of HKD 8,042.75 exceeds the HKD 6,000 cap by HKD 2,042.75. It is correctly excluded by the affordability gate. Among affordable offers the ranking by all-in cost is A2 (18,016.18), A4 (18,652.50), A1 (19,293.82).

Finding 3 - front-loaded plans. A4_EQUAL_PRINCIPAL has the smallest budget headroom (HKD 85.00) because its first payment (HKD 5,915.00) is much larger than its last (HKD 5,054.58). Using an average payment would have understated the affordability risk. A controlled changed-input test (C6, same principal, rate and term, only the repayment structure changed) confirms the structural effect: equal-principal interest HKD 11,081.50 vs annuity HKD 11,403.20. Comparing A4's interest directly with A2's would be invalid because their rates and fee structures differ.

Affordability limits. A bisection solver gives the largest principal whose peak instalment fits the HKD 6,000 ceiling: A1 HKD 199,595.65, A2 HKD 196,347.59, A3 HKD 134,282.36, A4 HKD 182,599.83. This turns a yes/no verdict into an actionable borrowing limit.

5. Evaluation and independent verification

The brief asks for a simple comparison or reference calculation, an independent check of an important result, and an honest account of failures and limitations. outputs/case_A/verification.md and outputs/fresh_case_B/verification.md hold the full 83-row pass/fail tables; evidence/evaluation_record.md is the compact Appendix F version.

5.1 Two genuinely different checking methods

The brief warns that "a self-check may repeat the original error; a second agent is not a substitute for a different checking method". verify.py therefore re-reads the saved schedule CSVs and the original offers CSV (not the engine's summary.json values) and uses different algorithms:

Quantity Engine Independent checker
Level payment closed-form annuity formula bisection on a recursive balance equation b_t = b_(t-1)(1+i) - p until b_n = 0
Monthly IRR bisection on NPV Newton's method with the analytic derivative dNPV/dr = sum t * o_t / (1+r)^(t+1)
Identities computed in memory recomputed from the saved schedule CSV rows

Cash-flow identities checked on saved numbers: interest_t = opening_t * i; closing_t = opening_t - principal_t; outflow_t = interest + principal + fee; sum of principal = original principal; final balance = 0; sum of outflows = interest + principal + all monthly fees; all-in cost = total interest + all fees = sum(outflows) - net disbursement; NPV(i_IRR) = 0; peak outflow and the affordability flag agree with the saved schedule. Maximum absolute residual observed was about 5e-11 currency units, against a 1e-6 tolerance.

5.3 What the evaluation establishes - and what it does not

It establishes internal arithmetic consistency, agreement between two different implementations of the payment and the IRR, correct behaviour under changed and invalid inputs, and that the ranking claim follows from the saved schedules. It does not establish that the synthetic offers are real quotations, that any lender withholds fees at t = 0, that a borrower's rate stays fixed, or that the conclusion generalises beyond these eight constructed offers. Recalculation cannot authenticate source data. The naive baseline is a deliberately simple heuristic chosen before seeing results; no rules were tuned after observing the test outcomes. Because the sample is small and self-constructed, these results are evidence of a working, checkable procedure, not of a market-wide empirical finding.

5.4 A real failure and its correction

The first independent verification run reported 11/12 checks passed with AssertionError: Newton IRR did not converge (verify.py exited 5). Printing the Newton iterates showed convergence in 3-4 steps - NPV fell from ~12,590 to ~1e-11 - but the stopping rule broke on abs(step) <= 1e-15 before accepting a small NPV residual, then raised. The fix was a relative convergence criterion (tol * net_disbursement, appropriate because NPV is measured in currency units) and returning the iterate when the Newton step becomes tiny. After the fix all 83 checks passed, both for Case A and for the fresh Case B. The episode is retained here and in docs/agent-workflow.md because it is the clearest evidence that the checker can actually fail and detect a problem, rather than being a decorative always-green script.

6. Limitations

  • Monthly instalments only. periods_per_year must be 12; quarterly or

daily products are refused with an explicit error. No intra-month compounding.

  • Fixed, simple products. No variable rates, early repayment, refinancing,

grace periods, balloon payments, insurance, default/prepayment risk or taxes.

  • Nominal all-in cost is undiscounted. It is not comparable across different

terms without the APR/EAR; the report shows both and notes where rankings differ (A3 is cheapest in nominal cost but unaffordable).

  • Fee timing is an assumption. Fees are withheld from the disbursement. A

lender who finances the fee instead produces different cash flows.

  • Synthetic data. No real quotes, no market data, no authenticity check.

currency is a display label; no FX conversion is performed, so Case A and Case B must never be compared against each other.

  • APR definition. The IRR-based borrower cost is not asserted to be any

jurisdiction's statutory APR (e.g. a regulated disclosure figure).

  • Numerical. Schedules are written at 10 decimals; residuals of ~1e-10

remain after round-tripping. Checks use a 1e-6 absolute tolerance.

  • Evaluation size. 83 checks on two small self-constructed cases; a short,

clearly defined comparison, not a benchmark suite. No timing or token measurements were instrumented, so none are reported.

  • Single agent. No team coordination was used; the reusable capability is a

regular Skill, not a Team Skill.
